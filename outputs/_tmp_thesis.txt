Abstract
Abstract
The cosmic-ray Sun shadow is a deficit of galactic cosmic rays observed in the
direction of the Sun, resulting from the combined effects of geometrical blocking by
the solar disk and magnetic deflection in the solar magnetic field. Since the trajectories
of charged cosmic rays near the Sun are sensitive to the magnetic field structure along
their propagation paths, the Sun shadow provides an important indirect probe of the
solar magnetic field, particularly in the corona and its extended regions.
The LHAASO-KM2A (Large High Altitude Air Shower Observatory⚶Kilometer
Square Array) detector, with its high statistics for cosmic rays above 10 TeV, enables
high-significance observationsof the Sun shadow on a daily timescale. In this work, we
perform a systematic study of the cosmic-ray Sun shadow using KM2A data collected
from 2020 to 2024. A comprehensive analysis framework is developed, including event
selection, energy reconstruction, solar position tracking, background estimation based
on Sun/Moon shadow methods, construction of two-dimensional deficit maps, shadow
fitting, and deficit calculation. The detector performance, including angular resolution,
pointing accuracy, and stability, is validated using the Moon shadow.
The results show that on a yearly timescale, the overall Sun shadow deficit de-
creases with increasing solar activity during 2020⚶2024. On a daily timescale, a signif-
icant periodic variation is observed in 2022, with a period of approximately25.9 days
(F-test significance of6.98 𝜎), consistent with the solar rotation period of∼ 27.3 days.
High-energy cosmic rays can penetrate deep into the solar atmosphere, reaching low
coronal heights and even near the photosphere, and are therefore modulated by mag-
netic fields from the photosphere, corona, and interplanetary space.
To investigate the origin of this variation, correlation analyses are performed be-
tween the Sun shadow deficit and multiple solar observables, including sunspot num-
bers, low-latitude photospheric magnetic fields, and near-Sun coronal magnetic fields.
The results indicate a positive correlation between sunspot number and Sun shadow
deficit. Usingthediscretecorrelationfunction(DCF),thecorrelationbetweenthedeficit
and the photospheric magnetic field is found to peak at zero time lag, with a maximum
correlation coefficient of0.63 ± 0.039, suggesting that solar rotation plays a key role in
the observed periodic modulation.
Furthercomparisonsamongmagneticfieldsatdifferentheightsshowthatthemax-
imumcorrelationcoefficientsbetweentheSunshadowdeficitandthemagneticfieldare
0.59 ± 0.024at the photosphere,0.44 ± 0.029at 1.10-1.15 𝑅𝑠, and0.51 ± 0.017at 1.20-
1.25 𝑅𝑠. This indicates that the correlation is strongest at the photospheric level and
becomes weaker in the low corona. Additionally, the energy dependence of the Sun

probe of solar magnetic field structures and their short-term evolution, offering new
insights into the study of coronal and interplanetary magnetic fields.
